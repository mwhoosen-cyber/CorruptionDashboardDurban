Transparency

Methodology & sources

How this dashboard is built, how cases are classified, and the rules we apply so that nothing here is overstated.

Read the full methodology

What this covers. The dashboard documents four overlapping failures: corruption (tender fraud, personal enrichment, criminal charges), financial misconduct (irregular, fruitless and wasteful expenditure), infrastructure failure (where neglected maintenance carried a measurable public cost), and governance instability. Wasteful expenditure is treated as a first-class category, not a footnote.

Sources. Every case is sourced from publicly available reporting and official documents, including:

  • Auditor-General of South Africa (AGSA) consolidated municipal audit reports (MFMA)
  • National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) statements, Hawks (DPCI) and Special Investigating Unit (SIU) records
  • The Judicial Commission of Inquiry into State Capture (Zondo Commission) reports
  • Public Protector South Africa reports and South African court records and judgments
  • The eThekwini Municipality budget and analysis by a former member of Parliament's Standing Committee on the Auditor-General (SCOAG)
  • Investigative journalism by Daily Maverick, amaBhungane, News24, City Press, GroundUp, TimesLive, Mail & Guardian, SABC News, the Mercury and others, plus international wires (CNN, Al Jazeera, Insurance Journal) on matters of global reach

Status labels. Cases are marked conservatively. "Alleged" means publicly reported but not yet criminally charged. "Charged" means formal NPA charges have been laid. "Convicted" means a court has handed down a guilty verdict (including civil court findings of constitutional violation or delinquent-director declarations). "Dismissed" means removed from office. "Ongoing" means matter is before court but unresolved.

One accountability bloc. This dashboard counts the ANC and the MK Party together as a single accountability bloc, and the headline totals, summary cards and charts treat them as one. The rationale is personnel overlap, not electoral alliance: the MK Party was founded in 2023 largely by former ANC members; Jacob Zuma led the ANC before founding and leading MK; Brian Molefe moved from Zuma-era state-owned enterprises into MK's leadership; and the state-capture-era appointees documented here served under the ANC. The two are distinct, rival parties at the ballot box, so this grouping is an explicit editorial framing — not a claim that they are one organisation. Because the bloc is treated as a single unit, the dashboard's aggregates — the hero totals, summary cards and every chart — are never split between ANC and MK, and there is no party filter. The case table does identify each individual's specific party (ANC or MK) and retains their sourced role and affiliation history, but the bloc's totals cannot be broken down or filtered by party.

Party attribution. Cases are attributed to the individual's current political party. Jacob Zuma and Brian Molefe — who served under the ANC but currently hold MK Party leadership positions — are listed as MK Party, with an explicit "Affiliation context" note clarifying that the alleged conduct occurred during their ANC tenure. Corruption under Zuma's presidency by people he appointed who have not moved to MK remain attributed to the ANC (the party they served under) but tagged as "National (Zuma-era)" in scope.

Currency of MK Party membership. Floyd Shivambu is not included in the case database because he was expelled from the MK Party on 8 August 2025 and is no longer a member. Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla is listed as a former MK Party MP — she resigned on 28 November 2025 amid the Russia-Ukraine recruitment scandal. The MK Instability section below documents these and other leadership changes with sources.

Scope. Each case is tagged either "eThekwini Metro" (local-government corruption and wasteful expenditure) or "National (Zuma-era)" / "National" (state-capture-era matters by Zuma or his presidential appointees, and conduct by current MK Party figures). Both are relevant to eThekwini voters because the political figures involved either currently lead a party contesting eThekwini or shaped the institutions that govern it.

Wasteful expenditure is captured across the "Financial Misconduct" category (fruitless & wasteful spending — interest, penalties, avoidable legal costs, excessive consultants, returned-grant funds), "Infrastructure Failure" (GO!Durban, sewage), and "Audit Outcome" (AGSA irregular-expenditure totals). The R8bn+ GO!Durban BRT case and the Moses Mabhida Stadium R520m repair programme are the largest single items.

Source URLs. Cases added in the most recent dataset refresh link directly to specific articles. Some older entries link to outlet homepages where the specific article URL is paywalled or archived — flag any broken links via the corrections process below.

Rand values are stated in nominal terms as reported by the source. Where a figure is in dispute or unclear, it is listed as null and excluded from totals.
